# EU AI Act Compliance Research: Record-Keeping and Logging (Article 12 & 19)

**Official Reference:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Articles 12 and 19
**Compliance Domain:** Automatic Logging and Record-Keeping for High-Risk AI Systems
**Criticality Level:** CRITICAL - Essential for Traceability and Accountability

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Articles 12 and 19 establish comprehensive logging requirements for high-risk AI systems. These systems must automatically record events throughout their lifetime to enable traceability, post-market monitoring, and accountability. Logs must be retained by providers and deployers for specified periods and made available to authorities.

---

## 2. Core Legal Requirements

### 2.1 Article 12: Automatic Logging Capabilities

**Article 12(1):** "High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed with capabilities enabling the automatic recording of events ('logs') over the lifetime of the system."

**Purpose of Logging (Article 12(2)):** The logging capabilities shall ensure a level of traceability of the functioning of the high-risk AI system that is appropriate to the intended purpose of the system, and in particular shall enable the recording of events relevant for:

a) **Identifying situations that may result in the high-risk AI system presenting a risk** or in a substantial modification
b) **Facilitating post-market monitoring** referred to in Article 72
c) **Monitoring the operation** of high-risk AI systems referred to in Article 26(5) (deployer obligations)

---

## 3. Required Logging Elements

### 3.1 General Logging Requirements

**All high-risk AI systems must log:**

1. **Event Timestamps**
   - Start date and time
   - End date and time
   - Event duration where applicable

2. **System Inputs**
   - Input data or references to input data
   - Input data characteristics (quality, completeness)
   - Source of inputs

3. **System Outputs**
   - Decision or recommendation generated
   - Confidence scores
   - Alternative recommendations considered

4. **Decision Context**
   - Relevant contextual information
   - System state at time of decision
   - Parameters and configurations active

5. **User Interactions**
   - User actions (approvals, overrides, rejections)
   - Human oversight activities
   - Manual interventions

6. **System Events**
   - Errors and exceptions
   - Warnings and alerts
   - Performance degradations
   - System updates or modifications

### 3.2 Special Requirements for Biometric Systems

**Article 12(3):** For high-risk AI systems referred to in point 1(a) of Annex III (remote biometric identification), logging capabilities shall provide, at a minimum:

a) **Recording of the period of each use** of the system (start date and time and end date and time of each use)
b) **The reference database** against which input data has been checked by the system
c) **The input data** for which the search has led to a match
d) **Identification of the natural persons** involved in the verification of the results, as referred to in Article 14(5)

**Additional biometric logging best practices:**
- Biometric template quality metrics
- Match scores and thresholds
- Liveness detection results (for face recognition)
- False accept/reject tracking

## 4. Log Retention Requirements

### 4.1 Provider Retention (Article 19(1))

**Article 19(1):** "Providers of high-risk AI systems shall keep the logs referred to in Article 12(1), automatically generated by their high-risk AI systems, to the extent such logs are under their control."

**Duration:** Logs must be retained for the period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system.

**Minimum retention period:** Not explicitly specified in Article 19(1), but should align with:
- Technical documentation retention (10 years per Article 18)
- Post-market monitoring requirements
- Applicable sector-specific regulations

### 4.2 Deployer Retention (Article 19(2))

**Article 19(2):** "Deployers of high-risk AI systems shall keep the logs automatically generated by that high-risk AI system to the extent such logs are under their control, for a period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, **of at least six months**, unless provided otherwise in applicable Union or national law, in particular in Union law on the protection of personal data."

**Minimum:** 6 months
**Typically:** Longer periods advisable based on:
- Risk level of the system
- Sector-specific requirements
- Litigation and investigation timelines
- Post-market monitoring needs

### 4.3 GDPR Alignment

**Important:** When logs contain personal data, retention must comply with GDPR principles:
- Data minimization (Article 5(1)(c) GDPR)
- Storage limitation (Article 5(1)(e) GDPR)
- Purpose limitation (Article 5(1)(b) GDPR)

**Recommendation:** Define retention periods balancing AI Act traceability requirements with GDPR minimization principles. Document the legal basis and necessity for retention periods chosen.

## 5. Log Content Specifications

### 5.1 Data Elements per Log Entry

**Minimum required fields:**

```
{
  "log_id": "unique identifier",
  "timestamp": "ISO 8601 timestamp",
  "event_type": "decision|error|override|update|...",
  "system_version": "version identifier",
  "input": {
    "data": "input data or reference",
    "quality_metrics": "data quality indicators",
    "source": "origin of input"
  },
  "output": {
    "decision": "system output",
    "confidence": "confidence score",
    "alternatives": "alternative outputs considered"
  },
  "context": {
    "user_id": "deployer user if applicable",
    "session_id": "session identifier",
    "environment": "deployment environment info"
  },
  "human_oversight": {
    "reviewer": "human who reviewed (if applicable)",
    "action": "approved|rejected|overridden",
    "justification": "reason for human decision"
  },
  "system_state": {
    "performance_metrics": "current accuracy, latency, etc.",
    "alerts_active": "any active alerts or warnings"
  }
}
```

### 5.2 Log Format Standards

**Recommended standards:**
- **Structured logging:** JSON, XML, or standardized formats
- **Machine-readable:** Enabling automated analysis
- **Human-readable:** Interpretable when needed
- **Immutable:** Append-only, tamper-evident
- **Timestamped:** High-precision timestamps (millisecond or better)
- **Searchable:** Indexed for efficient querying

## 6. Technical Implementation Requirements

### 6.1 Logging Infrastructure

**Required capabilities:**
- **Automatic logging:** No manual intervention required
- **Always-on:** Logging cannot be disabled in production
- **Failure resilience:** Logging failures don't crash system; alerts triggered if logging fails
- **High performance:** Minimal impact on system performance
- **Scalability:** Handles production-scale event volumes
- **Security:** Logs protected from unauthorized access and tampering

### 6.2 Log Storage

**Requirements:**
- **Persistent storage:** Survive system restarts and failures
- **Sufficient capacity:** Storage sized for retention period
- **Backup and recovery:** Logs backed up regularly
- **Access control:** Only authorized personnel can access logs
- **Encryption:** Logs encrypted at rest and in transit
- **Integrity protection:** Tamper detection (checksums, signatures)

### 6.3 Log Access and Retrieval

**Must support:**
- **Querying:** Search logs by time range, event type, user, etc.
- **Filtering:** Narrow results to relevant events
- **Export:** Extract logs for analysis or audits
- **Audit trail:** Log all access to logs themselves
- **APIs:** Programmatic access for monitoring and analysis

---

## 7. Traceability Requirements

**Article 12(2) specifies logging must enable traceability:**

### 7.1 Backward Traceability
- From a system output, trace back to:
  - Input data used
  - Model version and parameters
  - Decision logic applied
  - Context and environment
  - Human oversight actions

### 7.2 Forward Traceability
- From an event or modification, trace forward to:
  - Affected decisions and outputs
  - Downstream consequences
  - User impacts

### 7.3 Risk Event Traceability
- Identify events leading to risks
- Reconstruct sequence of events leading to incidents
- Analyze root causes of failures
- Support corrective action identification

## 8. Integration with Other Requirements

### 8.1 Post-Market Monitoring (Article 72)

Logs are essential data source for post-market monitoring:
- Performance trend analysis
- Incident detection and investigation
- Accuracy drift monitoring
- User interaction patterns

### 8.2 Human Oversight (Article 14 & 26)

Logs support human oversight by:
- Providing data for monitoring dashboards
- Enabling retrospective review of decisions
- Recording human interventions for analysis
- Detecting automation bias patterns

### 8.3 Risk Management (Article 9)

Logs support risk management by:
- Identifying risk events in production
- Validating risk mitigation effectiveness
- Detecting new risks emerging post-deployment

### 8.4 Incident Reporting (Article 73)

Logs are critical for serious incident reporting:
- Incident detection
- Root cause analysis
- Impact assessment
- Corrective action development

## 9. Common Non-Compliance Patterns

### 9.1 Critical Failures

❌ **No logging capability**
- System has no automatic logging implemented

❌ **Insufficient logging**
- Only errors logged, not decisions
- Logs lack required information (no inputs, outputs, or context)
- Biometric systems missing special logging requirements

❌ **Inadequate retention**
- Logs deleted before minimum retention period
- No log retention policy

❌ **Inaccessible logs**
- Logs exist but cannot be queried or retrieved
- No export or analysis capability

❌ **Insecure logs**
- Logs unencrypted
- No access controls
- No tamper protection

### 9.2 Minor Deficiencies

⚠️ **Incomplete log data**
- Some required fields missing
- Inconsistent logging across system components

⚠️ **Poor log structure**
- Unstructured text logs (hard to analyze)
- Inconsistent formats

⚠️ **Performance issues**
- Logging degrades system performance significantly
- Log storage insufficient for retention period

## 10. Best Practices

### 10.1 Logging Strategy

1. **Log at appropriate level:** Balance detail with performance and storage
2. **Structured logging:** Use consistent, machine-readable formats
3. **Centralized logging:** Aggregate logs from distributed components
4. **Real-time streaming:** Enable real-time monitoring alongside historical analysis
5. **Sampling for high-volume systems:** Log all critical events; sample routine events if necessary (document sampling methodology)

### 10.2 Log Management

1. **Automated log rotation:** Manage log files efficiently
2. **Compression:** Compress archived logs to save storage
3. **Tiered storage:** Move older logs to cheaper storage
4. **Retention automation:** Automatically delete logs after retention period (per policy)
5. **Regular log audits:** Verify logging is working correctly

### 10.3 Recommended Tools and Technologies

**Logging Frameworks:**
- Python: logging module, structlog, loguru
- Java: Log4j, SLF4J, Logback
- Node.js: Winston, Bunyan

**Log Management Platforms:**
- ELK Stack (Elasticsearch, Logstash, Kibana)
- Splunk
- Datadog
- Grafana Loki
- AWS CloudWatch, Azure Monitor, Google Cloud Logging

**Database Options:**
- Time-series databases (InfluxDB, TimescaleDB)
- NoSQL databases (MongoDB, Cassandra)
- Data warehouses (BigQuery, Snowflake)

✅ Automatic logging implemented
✅ Logging covers all required event types
✅ Logs include all mandatory data elements
✅ Biometric systems include special logging requirements (if applicable)

✅ Log retention policy documented
✅ Minimum retention periods met (6 months for deployers)

✅ GDPR compliance for personal data in logs

✅ Log storage sufficient for retention period
✅ Logs encrypted at rest and in transit
✅ Access controls implemented and tested

## 12. Code-Level Indicators

**✅ Compliance Indicators:**
- Structured logging framework integrated
- Logging statements throughout codebase at key decision points
- Log schema defined and documented
- Log retention and rotation configured
- Logging configuration in version control
- Automated tests for logging functionality

**❌ Non-Compliance Indicators:**
- No logging code
- Only ad-hoc print statements for debugging
- Logging only in development, disabled in production
- No log retention policy or configuration
- Logs stored in temporary locations
- No log access controls

---

## 13. Penalties and Consequences

**Administrative Fines (Article 99):**
- Up to €15,000,000 or 3% of worldwide annual turnover for violations of logging requirements

**Operational Consequences:**
- Cannot investigate incidents without logs
- Cannot demonstrate compliance without audit trail
- Liability risks if incidents occur and logs unavailable
- Post-market monitoring impossible without logs

---

## 14. Key Takeaways

1. **Automatic logging is mandatory** - not optional or nice-to-have
2. **Logs must be comprehensive** - covering inputs, outputs, decisions, human oversight
3. **Retention is legally required** - minimum 6 months for deployers, longer advisable
4. **Traceability is the goal** - logs must enable understanding what happened and why
5. **Security matters** - logs must be protected like any sensitive data
6. **Plan infrastructure** - logging at scale requires proper architecture and storage
